# Stage 3 Design: Skins

Stage 3 of the roadmap (design.md "Roadmap") is the look and effects of the play screen. This file plans the skin system: the runtime model the player draws from, the importers that make existing skins playable, tapestry's own skin format, and its export to beatoraja-family players.

**Status.** Decided; the decisions that change design.md are in it ("Goals", "Rendering and Skins", "Custom Skin Format", "beatoraja Lua Skins", "Extension Boundaries", "Settings Scopes", "Language and Crate Structure", "Libraries", "Roadmap", "Open Questions"). The fast and slow counts in `Tally` go into design.md with the evaluator change that adds them (S9). This file holds the plan and the details, and is removed when stage 3 exits.

**Citations.** `file:N` points into the repository at commit 17e17de unless it names another revision (8622dd7 for the canvas, regions and lanes of S5, c613b6f for the number, judgment and gauge widgets of S5b), and design.md is cited by section name. "ED" is LR2oraja Endless Dream; "upstream" is beatoraja itself. What is said about their skin loaders was read from their published source as a description of the file format; none of their code is ported (design.md "Libraries").

**What the skin system must do:**
- **Existing skins first.** Skins made for beatoraja-family players become playable in tapestry before tapestry's own format exists. The importers are separate from the format and never shape it.
- **A small format of its own.** Few people make skins today. A spec that is large, or has features an author gains nothing from, goes unused. Every concept an author learns has to pay off for them.
- **One source, two skins.** A skin written in tapestry's format also builds a beatoraja skin, so one piece of work reaches the players of both. The beatoraja skin is a faithful subset: tapestry's may do more, and what beatoraja cannot express degrades in the export and never breaks it.
- **Not bound to 16:9.** The format adapts to other aspect ratios such as 21:9. The beatoraja export stays 16:9.
- **The player adjusts the composition.** A home setup is not a cabinet. Players move their monitor or where they sit because a skin fixes the whole layout and the existing players can only stretch the screen. Here the player moves and resizes the parts of the screen, and the skin author does nothing for it.
- **Crisp at any size.** Image skins blur or get jaggy when stretched or shrunk. Scaling is handled at load, so it costs nothing per frame.

**Direction applied throughout:**
- **Two levels.** A broad compiled *runtime model* is what the player draws from; the importers and the compiler of the small *authored format* both target it. The runtime model grows only with the PR that first needs a feature, which for import-only features is an importer.
- **The authored format is specified on its own terms.** Its semantics are written for tapestry; the beatoraja export is a constraint every construct must meet (it has an export rule), never the source of the semantics.
- **The logic thread never knows skins exist.** Everything a skin shows comes from the play view, the effect queue and the chart, as today (design.md "Rendering and Skins").
- **Nothing is built ahead of the PR that needs it,** and skin work before stage 2 exits lives in new portable crates that touch neither `render`, `player`, `game` nor `eval`. S10 is the exception, by the owner's decision: its PRs land one by one before stage 2 exits and before S7, each gated by Windows CI.

## 1. Scope

**In:**
- **The runtime model and its evaluation** in portable crates: elements, keyframe tracks, timers, typed expressions, regions, widgets, the atlas.
- **The beatoraja importer** (JSON and Lua skins) into the runtime model, before the authored format.
- **The authored format**, its compiler and diagnostics.
- **The beatoraja exporter** and an authoring CLI that checks, previews, and builds both skins in one command.
- **Player layout adjustment**: per-region position, scale and visibility, lane width, and an adjust mode drawn with the play screen's sprites.
- **The standard skin**, written in the format with self-made art, and the removal of `SimpleSkin`.
- **BGA**, starting from the stage 0 spike's decoder.

**Out:**
- **The LR2 importer**, which comes right after stage 3 (§7, decision 1).
- **Graphs and visualizers** (score graph, judgment graph, hit-error), double play, mouse dragging in the adjust mode (it comes with `egui`), SVG images, SDF shapes, MSDF text.
- **Other screens' skins** (song selection, the song decision screen, results). Stage 3 skins the play screen only; the decision screen, planned after S10-07, has a built-in presentation (§6, S10).

## 2. The runtime model (`skin-model`)

### R1. Elements, tracks and draw order

A compiled skin is a list of elements in draw order. Each element has a source (an image frame, an image choice (R6), a number, text, a bar, or a widget), a keyframe track, a timer, a visibility condition, a blend, a region and the covers it follows (`Follow`: none, LIFT, SUDDEN+ or both; R6, "Followers"). The runtime model carries the union of what imported skins need: five blends (alpha, add, subtract, multiply, invert), quadratic and cubic eases, per-object or per-keyframe ease, clip rectangles, and derived edge timers. Each of these enters with the PR that first needs it: an importer PR for import-only features, the LR2 importer for the LR2-only ones. The lanes' bar line groups are parts of the lanes, not elements: each has its own track, timer, visibility, blend and placement and draws before the notes (R6).

**Draw order across blends.** The runtime model's `QuadBatch` is one ordered list with preallocated runs; a run breaks wherever the blend, the clip rectangle or a cover paint's inline trim clip (`Run::clip_rect`, R6 "Cover paint") changes, so two neighbouring elements with different clips never share a scissor. Render's `SpriteBatch` is that batch (S10-03b-1), and the renderer draws the runs in order, each under its clip's scissor (S10-01, S10-02), so an alpha element over an additive one is drawn over it (§6, "Render integration"). The CPU preview applies the same runs.

### R2. Animation

Times are integer milliseconds in files and microseconds inside. For an element with timer `T` and keyframes at `k0 < … < kn`:
- The element is not drawn while `T` is off.
- Local time is `t = now − start(T)`; an element without a timer uses the built-in `screen` timer, which starts at the loading lifetime's origin while the play screen loads (R3, the loading runtime) and at `ready`'s start in a play, so it is drawn from the screen's start as the target loaders draw untimed objects, and its animation starts again at the countdown (D3). An element authored on `ready` stays off while loading.
- After the last key, one of three behaviors, named by the author: **hold** (keep the last key's values), **hide** (drawn while `t ≤ kn`, not after), or **loop from `L`** (`k0 ≤ L < kn`): for `t > L`, `t' = L + (t − L) mod (kn − L)`, so `t = kn` already shows `L`. This matches the target loaders exactly (ED `SkinObject.prepareRegion`), so the export is one-to-one.
- Before `k0` the element is not drawn.
- Between keys, x, y, w, h, alpha, r, g, b and angle interpolate; a field left out of a key keeps the previous key's value. A key's `(x, y)` is the rect's top-left corner. The target loaders' destinations are bottom-left and y-up, in the skin's own size, scaled by the output size over it; the beatoraja importer scales and reflects them (§3, "Mapping"). Each element names a 9-point pivot, a point of the sampled rect (corner, edge midpoint or centre), about which it rotates: with x = 100 and w from 40 to 80 the corner stays and a centre pivot moves from 120 to 140. The angle is in degrees and interpolates numerically (350 to 10 passes 180); drawing reduces it into `[0, 2π)` (`rem_euclid` before the conversion, so large keys keep their precision). `Quad` carries the pivot as a canvas point and the angle in radians.
- Ease per track: linear, in, out, step (in and out are quadratic). Cubic and per-keyframe ease come into the runtime model with the importer that needs them.
- **Image frames** are not part of the track: an image's frame cycle runs on the image's own timer, so an element can show on `ready` while its frames restart on `judge`. With `n` frames over a period `p` (1 µs to the longest key time), `t` after that timer started shows frame `((t mod p) · n) / p`; frame 0 shows while the timer is off, without a cycle, or with one frame, as in the target loaders (`SkinSourceImage.java:73-89`). Both timers come from the same frame's fill, before any element is drawn. Each entry of an image choice (R6) cycles over its own frames on its own timer in the same way, so switching entries never restarts a clock.
- **Bar groups** (R6) sample their tracks, visibility and image cycles as an element does, on the frame's `now` and their timers' starts; the display offset moves only the bars' travel, so through a STOP a bar holds still while its animation runs, and before `play` starts a group on `ready` animates while one on `play` stays off.
- In the authored format, keys must be strictly increasing from `k0 ≥ 0` and a loop point must lie inside the track (the target loaders hide a finished track by setting its time to −1, which a negative key would make visible again); anything else is a load error. The target loaders accept unsorted and equal-time keys and apply a loop only when the last key is after 0; the importer reproduces that, and the exporter emits only tracks that need neither. Tests pin `k0`, `kn` and `L` at exactly and ±1 µs, for hold, hide, loop and a single-key track.

### R3. State and timers

The skin reads a per-frame `SkinFrame`, filled from the render thread's `FrameInput`, the chart, a play context given to `prepare`, and a reducer over the drained effects. The play context is fixed for one attempt: the preset's gauge descriptors (`start`, `max`, the gauge's `look` and its `border`, in the order of `PlayView::gauges`, which carry values only, crates/game/src/render.rs:17-28), the attempt's start position and, in practice, its section, so the lanes widget draws only the section's targets (stage2.md D15) and `ready` and `play` start from the attempt's own countdown, not chart time 0, the preset's default long-note mode (`ln_default`), which with the chart's own mode decides whether an imported skin draws a long note's end cap, and the end of the attempt's notes on the play clock (`last_note_end`, the source's `E`: the last note's time in a normal play, the last timeline's in autoplay, and in practice `(endtime + 1000) · 100 / freq`, `play/BMSPlayer.java:199, 587`). Each `SkinFrame` says whether its update entered the play in its playing state (`playing`): false during the countdown and after a stop, the finish or the fade-out, and still true when a gauge fails in that same update.

**Where each kind of value comes from:**
- **Numbers come only from the play view.** A dropped effect loses a flash, never a number on screen (crates/game/src/render.rs:36-40). Counts, EX score, BP, combo, gauges and the like are read from `PlayView`, never accumulated from effects.
- **Edge timers come from ordered effects.** A timer's value is its start time, taken from the event's chart time, not the frame's. `last_note` is the exception: it is sampled, not started by an effect.
- **Lane geometry is resolved once per frame** from `SkinFrame::lanes` (a loading frame's lanes while loading), before any element is drawn, whether or not the lanes draw (`LaneFrameGeometry`): the lift's, SUDDEN+'s and HIDDEN+'s lengths, the line and the scroll span. The lanes, the cover paints (each resolving its own trim line from it, R6 "Trim"), followers, bar groups and grades all read that one result, so nothing shows the previous frame's covers. Bar lines travel from the chart position at the display head (`now + display_offset`), as the notes do, while the bar groups' clocks stay on `now` (R2); the prepared chart's bar lines are not section targets, and a section's `prepare` resets the groups' timers with the others.
- **Score rates come from the snapshot and the attempt** (S6c-02). `rate.current_bp` is `⌊10 000 · EX / (2 · judged)⌋` and `rate.whole_bp` the same over the attempt's targets (the reducer's, prepared from the chart and the attempt: one per tap or long note, mines excluded, a practice section's own), each 10 000 when its denominator is 0. EX is the snapshot's `Tally::ex_score`; `judged` is PERFECT + GREAT + GOOD + BAD + POOR, less the empty POORs when the play context says the preset counts them as POOR (`PlayContext::empty_poor_counted`, from `preset.empty_poor.counted`), since the evaluator then adds each to both counts. The sums are taken in `u64` before the field's range, which is 0 to `i32::MAX`: a custom EX weight can pass 10 000 and nothing caps it. Holding a long note, a mine, BP or the combo never moves a denominator, and a frame's rates and the `when` timers reading them follow the snapshot even ahead of its effects. A snapshot with more counted empty POORs than POORs is not the evaluator's: `Skin::build` returns `BuildError::InconsistentTally` before applying any effect, leaving the batch, reducer, fields and timers unchanged, and the `Play` is poisoned, returning the same error to every later `build` until `prepare` (the drained effects are not replayed, so continuing would show stale state).
- **The gauge kind comes from the shown descriptor** (S6c-02): `gauge.kind` is the look of the descriptor `SkinFrame::gauge` selects, the same `GaugeDesc::look` the gauge widget draws (R6), and `gauge.valid` is true only while both that descriptor and the snapshot's gauge at that index exist; otherwise the kind reads `normal`. A switch of the shown gauge changes both in that frame, without an effect. During loading the definition is the loading runtime's (below): `gauge.kind` is the look of the loading frame's descriptor and `gauge.valid` means that the frame gives one.
- **Play readouts come from prepared chart data and the current settings** (S6c-03, design.md "Play readouts"), never accumulated from effects, inferred from painted covers or recovered from the scroll map's STOP slopes. `prepare` takes the original 1× chart's BPM range, its initial BPM included, from the play context (`PlayContext::bpm_range`), and the attempt's end: the chart's last resolved object (the latest note end, mines included, or background sound start, at least 0; no sound tails, no margin) or the section's end, both in real time at the practice speed. Each frame carries `SkinFrame::readouts`: the playback tempo at the display head (`now + display_offset`), the hi-speed, the scroll policy's nominal travel duration as an exact ratio in ms, STOP waiting excluded, and the stored cover settings. The clocks are actual time from the attempt's start, `time.remaining_s` by ceiling and `time.elapsed_s` by floor, in `i128` and bounded afterwards; the countdown holds them at the attempt's length and 0, and neither the display offset nor completion moves them. The duration is rounded half up exactly, and the green value is `⌊3·ms/5⌋` of the rounded duration. `prepare` refuses a section ending before its start (`PrepareError::InvalidSectionRange`) and a range out of order or past `i32::MAX − 1` (`PrepareError::InvalidBpmRange`). `build` refuses a readout past `i32::MAX − 1`, a cover setting above 1 000, a zero denominator, or stored covers that are not what the lanes paint (`BuildError::InvalidReadouts`), after the tally check and before any effect; the `Play` is poisoned as for `InconsistentTally`, every later `build` returning the first error until `prepare`.
- **Conditions sample once per frame** after the drained effects are applied. Because the newest snapshot can be ahead of a drain that the effect buffer splits over frames (crates/player/src/render_side.rs:415-421), a condition may see a newer combo beside an older judgment for a frame; this is accepted and documented, and edge timers never derive from snapshot values that effects already carry.

**Feedback is not scoring.** A long note's `Judged` comes at its release or end, so feedback follows the existing play screen rules (crates/render/src/simple.rs:265-335):
- A press feedback (bomb, judgment display) comes from `Judged` at a press, or from `LnHeld` for a long note's head, never from the long note's completion; a completed long note adds its judgment text but no second bomb (the test at simple.rs:1013 is kept).
- A BAD head starts the visual hold through its `Judged`, without `LnHeld`; the hold ends at `LnHoldEnded`.
- `EmptyPoor` drives the empty-POOR feedback.

**Timers in stage 3:** `play`, `ready`, `failed` (from `GaugeState::failed_at`, crates/eval/src/gauge.rs:29, not from a gauge value: a normal gauge at 0 has not failed), `last_note` (sampled: it starts at the first frame with `playing` whose play-clock time `t` satisfies `E < t ≤ E + 5000` in whole ms, recording that frame's time, as the source checks it each frame, `play/BMSPlayer.java:66, 683-697`), `full_combo`, `gauge_max` (off until `play` starts, `play/BMSPlayer.java:639`), `beat` (from the scroll map, on the display clock: the latest integer-beat crossing, which a STOP does not restart); `judge`, `judge.<grade>` (only the latest judgment's grade is on), `combo.up` (the field `combo` holds the count); per lane `press`, `release` (off until the play view carries lane input, S9), `bomb` (on a press judged up to the skin's `bomb_up_to` grade, empty POOR included; never with `None`), `hold`, `mine`; `screen`, the untimed elements' timer, at `ready`'s start in a play and at the loading lifetime's origin while loading; `loading`, at that origin while loading and off in a play; and `when(<condition>)` timers, which start on the condition's rising edge and turn off on its falling edge. A `when` condition may read state and built-in timers but not another `when` timer, so there is no cycle and no order to choose; every `when` timer is updated once per frame before any element is drawn. `gauge_max` reads off while the snapshot's gauge is below its maximum; if a below-maximum effect is dropped and the gauge later refills, it keeps the earlier start. Every timer has a fixed slot sized in `prepare`. The reducer is reset in `prepare`, so a retry starts clean. Its tests split one sequence of effects across drains at every point and compare against explicit expected states, and cover a mid-chart countdown, a practice round and two elements sharing one `when` timer.

**The loading runtime** (S10-03c-1, design.md "Rendering and Skins", loading state). `Skin::prepare_loading` makes a loading state from the compiled skin with no chart: its own fields, built-in timer starts and readings, `when` starts and readings, image-cycle state and loading text slots, and no notes, reducer, effects or `Play`. `Skin::build_loading` fills it from a `LoadingFrame` (`now` on the loading clock, the lifetime's origin as `LoadState::since`, the percent clamped to 0..=100, the target, the layout, the lanes, the stored covers and the descriptor of the shown gauge, `LoadingGauge`: look, maximum, border and its index among the preset's gauges, no start or value) and draws with the play's own field and timer evaluation and element drawing, so nothing is frozen at an initial value. The origin is the moment the play screen begins showing its loading state (today the moment a chart is chosen). At `now − since = 250 000 µs`, `loading` reads 250 ms; a progress of 40 reads 40. The lanes draw their judgment line from the frame's geometry with no notes or bar lines; the gauge draws its empty frame (every segment unlit, its below-border images below the border) from the descriptor and nothing without one; cover paint follows the frame's covers; judgment feedback never draws. Text slots draw what was installed in them (`Skin::install_loading_text`, `Skin::install_loading_bitmap_text`) and nothing once cleared (`Skin::clear_loading_text`), a set of slots named by the chart fields they show, a composite with its parts; installing or clearing never touches a timer. The build allocates nothing and returns `Result<(), BuildError>`, refusing stored covers that `Play::build` would refuse (`InvalidReadouts`); after a refusal the state builds no more. Every PLAY field and built-in timer has one class for loading (`skin_model::loading_field`, `loading_timer`):

| Class | Fields and timers | While loading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live | `load.percent`; the timers `loading` and `screen`; every `when` timer | From the loading frame; a `when` timer starts on its condition's rising edge |
| Setting | `cover.permille`, `lift.permille`, `hidden.permille`, `cover.absolute_permille` (the white numbers), `cover.enabled`, `lift.enabled`, `hidden.enabled`, `cover.adjusting` | The frame's stored covers |
| Descriptor | `gauge.kind`, `gauge.valid` | Available while the frame gives a descriptor: the kind its look, `gauge.valid` true |
| Off | `ready`, `play`, `failed`, `last_note`, `full_combo`, `gauge_max`, `beat`, `judge`, `judge.<grade>`, `combo.up`, and per lane `press`, `release`, `bomb`, `hold`, `mine` | Known off: read as off, never unavailable |
| Unavailable | `combo`, `max_combo`, `ex_score`, `bp`, the `count.*` fields, `notes`, `gauge`, `rate.current_bp`, `rate.whole_bp`, the `bpm.*` fields, `time.remaining_s`, `time.elapsed_s`, `hispeed.x100`, `hispeed.valid`, `duration.ms`, `duration.green`, `duration.valid` | Not read |

Literals and the chosen chart's text slots are eligible. Since a program reads a missing field as its lower bound, unavailability is explicit: each final folded program's read set (both surviving arms of a conditional) is taken when the skin is loaded, and a program reading an unavailable field is rejected for loading, its number drawing nothing, its condition false, its selector drawing nothing and its `when` condition false, which clears a start it had. A program reading a descriptor field is checked each frame, so a descriptor that arrives after preparation lets it draw from that frame on. The gauge's value is unavailable whatever `gauge.valid` says. A play's frames are unchanged: `loading` stays off and `load.percent` reads 100.

**Lane input in the play view** *(coordination PR, §6 S9)*. Key beams and key pads need presses without notes and releases, which no effect carries: `PlaySound` comes only for a press with a target note that has a sound (crates/eval/src/evaluator.rs:927-937). `PlayView` gains, per lane, whether it is held now and the chart times of its latest press and release, filled from the same conversion that feeds the evaluator, so a scratch reversal (a release and a press at one time, crates/eval/src/convert.rs:386-390) and a control held at the start without a press edge (convert.rs:145-150) come out right. Autoplay fills it too. It is generic input state, not skin state.

**Fast/slow** need counts the play view does not have (`Tally`, crates/eval/src/evaluator.rs:122-136): they are added to `Tally` in `eval` (§7, decision 6).

### R4. Expressions

Expressions give conditions, values and image selections (R6); they never move or color anything (§4).
- **Types:** `bool`, `int`, and enumerations (judgment grades, gauge kinds: `gauge.kind` takes `assist_easy`, `easy`, `normal`, `hard`, `ex_hard` and `hazard`). An enumeration compares for equality and inequality with its own variants; it has no arithmetic or ordering and never compares with an integer. Durations are written `120ms` or `1.5s`. There is no float and no fixed point: values that are fractions are integers in a stated unit (the gauge and the covers in ‰, the score rate in basis points (10 000 is 100 %, R3), BPM and hi-speed ×100, the travel duration in ms), and only an image's on-screen geometry is float, after evaluation.
- **Readout units and availability** (R3): `bpm.current_x100`, `bpm.min_x100`, `bpm.max_x100` and `hispeed.x100` are floored hundredths, `duration.ms` and `duration.green` ms, the cover fields ‰, `time.remaining_s` and `time.elapsed_s` whole seconds, and the switches `bool`. A value the play may not have carries a validity field (`bpm.current_valid`, `bpm.range_valid`, `hispeed.valid`, `duration.valid`): unknown, it reads 0 with its validity false, which is distinct from a known 0 (a tempo below 0.01 BPM, an enabled cover at 0). An imported number showing it is drawn only under its validity, so it draws nothing while the value is unknown (§3).
- **Range-checked integers.** Every state field declares its range, and the compiler bounds every subexpression; an expression that could leave `[−2^31, 2^31 − 1]`, the range of the target's integer properties, is a compile error. Times read by expressions are in milliseconds (`elapsed` is milliseconds, −1 when off and at most 4 h, so a chart's time range fits and `elapsed(t) * 100` stays in range). State reads clamp to the declared range, so a value outside it can never break a bound the compiler proved. Within that bound the native evaluator and the exported Lua, whose numbers are Java `int` or `double`, compute the same results, so arithmetic needs no saturation. `/` and `%` floor and yield 0 for a zero divisor; the export lowers them to guarded prelude functions, since `math.floor(a / b)` alone does not give 0. Tests run both divisor signs and zero through the exported Lua as well as the native evaluator.
- **These rules are the authored language's.** The importer converts a Lua callback only when the native operations give the same result as Lua's: a fractional division whose result is compared (`n / 2 > 0` is true at 1 in Lua and false under floor division) is not converted and falls under §3's unsupported-callback rule.
- **Operators and built-ins:** `+ - * / %`, comparisons, `&& || !`, `if c then a else b`, `min`, `max`, `clamp`, `abs`, `on(timer)`, `elapsed(timer)`, and option tests.
- **Pipeline:** a hand-written lexer and Pratt parser with spans, names resolved against a static schema (name → type, range, slot, export mapping), type and range checks, folding of options and constants, and a postfix program with its stack depth computed. The evaluator runs it on a fixed stack, allocates nothing, and is in `IN_PLAY`.
- **Bounds** (design.md threat model "Skins"): source length (1024 bytes), nesting depth (an operator height of 64, counting `clamp` as its lowering; checked while parsing, before recursion), operations per expression (256) and per skin (65536), and stack depth (32) are capped, so per-frame cost has a bound known at load.
- **Rejected:** `evalexpr` (AGPL since 12.0, allocates), `rhai` and `cel-interpreter` (dynamic, allocating), `fasteval` (unmaintained, float), `exmex` (untyped). A combinator library adds nothing a ~300-line Pratt parser needs.

### R5. Layout: canvas, regions and player adjustment

**Canvas.** The canvas is 1080 reference pixels tall and `1080 × aspect` wide; narrower than 16:9 it is 1920 wide and grows taller instead (§7, decision 3). The aspect is capped at 4:1 (4320 wide) and 1:2 (3840 tall), with bars beyond; the branch is decided in integers, so a target just narrower than 16:9 grows taller, never narrower. It is scaled uniformly to the render target. At 16:9 every coordinate is exactly what the author wrote, so authors keep drawing at 1920×1080. `Skin::canvas` computes it from the target size; a skin with `Fit::Fixed` (imports) uses 1920×1080 pillarboxed or letterboxed, as render's letterbox does today (crates/render/src/renderer.rs:681 at 8622dd7).

**Regions** are a fixed set defined by the format, so the player sees the same controls on every skin:

| Region | Holds | Player adjusts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `playfield` | The lanes widget and every per-lane element, automatically | x, y, scale, lane width |
| `judge` | The judgment and combo widget, automatically; placed relative to `playfield` | x, y, scale, visible |
| `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom` | HUD elements the author tags | x, y, scale, visible (when marked optional) |
| `screen` | Everything untagged, backgrounds (`fill = cover`, `stretch` or `none`) | nothing |

- Each region has a 9-point anchor; on a wider or taller canvas it shifts by `(i + fx·(cw − 2i − 1920), fy·(ch − 1080))` for anchor fractions `fx`, `fy` and side inset `i`. The player's inset keeps edge regions near the centre on very wide screens; it is clamped to `max(0, (cw − 1920) / 2)` each frame and never moves `screen`.
- A region's box is the union of its elements' key rects (unrotated; for an image choice, every present entry's track), computed at load; the point of its anchor in that box is its scale pivot. The box, its pivot and the clamp take the authored rects as if nothing followed the covers, so a moving cover never moves another element. A bar group (R6) stands in the lanes' region with its own pivot, binding, native size and `Follow`, as an image does; neither its keys nor its bars' travel enter the box.
- The transform order is: author coordinates → the follow translation (R6, "Followers") → lane width (playfield, horizontal) → region scale about the anchor point → region translation → anchor shift → canvas to target. A `fill` background on `screen` (`cover` or `stretch`) skips the regions and fills the canvas. `judge` is placed relative to `playfield`: it is scaled about its own anchor and translated by its own adjustment first, then moved by the playfield's translation and anchor shift, once, so the inherited move is never scaled; it does not take the playfield's scale or lane width. A test with the judge at a scale other than 100 % moves the playfield and checks the judge moves exactly as far.
- **Ranges and the clamp.** Adjustments are integers: offsets within ±4320 reference pixels, scale and lane width 50–200 %, inset ≤ 1200; `Layout` refuses anything else. Each frame the translation is clamped per axis so that the region's logical box (its box after lane width, scale, translation and anchor shift) overlaps the canvas by at least `min(extent, 64)` pixels. `judge` and `playfield` are clamped jointly: with `P` the playfield's range and `J` the judge's (its inherited anchor shift included), the judge's own offset is clamped to `[Jlo − Phi, Jhi − Plo]`, then the playfield's to `P ∩ (J − local)`, so the judge still moves exactly as far as the playfield. A range whose author placement overlaps less keeps 0. The clamp does not promise that rotated geometry or a pivot stays on screen; `visible` is the control for hiding.
- **Controls.** `Skin::controls` says what the skin offers per region (this table; lane width only with the lanes in `playfield`, none for a region without elements or under `Fit::Fixed`); the layout's value for a control the skin does not offer is ignored.
- **Lane width** scales lane-bound elements horizontally about the playfield's anchor; nine-slice frames keep their borders.
- **Judgment-line height** is the `playfield` y: the whole field moves, its top included, so the visible time is unchanged. Lift, which shortens the lane and changes the visible range, stays a play setting (design.md "Algorithms").
- **Storage:** layout settings outside the skin, keyed by skin and aspect class (16:9, 21:9, 32:9, 16:10), so a skin update keeps them; a reset returns a region to the author's layout; they are device settings, one file per skin under the data root's `layouts\` (§7, decision 2).
- **Adjusting:** an adjust mode on the ready screen. A key or the turntable selects a region, arrows or the turntable move it, a modifier scales it, and region outlines are drawn with sprites, so no `egui` is needed. The values are also editable in the settings file.
- **Imported skins** use `Fit::Fixed`: one `screen` region, pillarboxed at other aspects, never stretched. They get no composition adjustment, since `screen` has no controls.

**Resize.** Today `prepare` gets the GPU and the chart and `build` gets no viewport (crates/render/src/skin.rs:14-34, 63-66). `prepare` gains the target's pixel size, from which it picks @2x images and rasterizes text at final size, and `FrameInput` gains the target size, from which the skin computes the canvas each frame (`SkinFrame::target`), so a resize during play recomputes anchors and the canvas fit without allocating. Assets chosen by pixel size stay as prepared until the next `prepare`, which runs between plays; a scale change in the adjust mode takes effect on screen at once and in the assets at the next `prepare`. Tests cover two pixel sizes of one aspect, and a resize during a prepared play followed by the next `prepare`.

**Rejected:** free per-element offsets (every skin would invent its own and players would face a different set per skin), constraint or flex layout (hard to learn, no export), per-element editing (players do not think in elements), non-uniform stretch (distorts).

### R6. Widgets

Built in, because they carry player logic or the scroll math, which stays in the player:
- `lanes` (S5): notes, long-note head, active and inactive bodies, tail, mines, the judgment line, one image per part and lane, and bar line groups ("Bar line groups"). What SUDDEN+, LIFT and HIDDEN+ paint is elements of their own ("Cover paint").
  - **Geometry.** Explicit lane rects `[x, w]` with an authored scratch side, the scratch outside every key. On the other side, the scratch moves to `span_start + span_end − x − w` and the key block to the near end, each key keeping its offset inside the block. Elements bound to a lane (`Bind::Lane`) or to the span (`Bind::Lanes`) map their x and w from the authored lane to the current one; `Skin::placement` takes the scratch side, which `build` reads from the attempt (`PlayContext::scratch`).
  - **Cover and lift.** The lift is in ‰ of the lane length and the cover in ‰ of the length left above the lift, each up to 1000, so together they may hide the whole lane. The lane length is `line − top` for the authored lanes (`LaneStyle::Simple`) and the source's lane height for an imported skin (`LaneStyle::Source`). The lanes paint no cover. The scroll span is the uncovered length and the lift raises the judgment line. With no span left nothing is scaled: the authored lanes draw the line, and an imported skin's also each tap and mine yet to come at the judgment position, within the frame's budget. These `LaneView` amounts are the effective ones: the configured amount while its cover is on and 0 while it is off. The configured amounts, kept while a cover is off, and the switches are readouts (R3) that nothing paints, and `build` refuses a frame where the two disagree.
  - **Hidden cover.** HIDDEN+ (`LaneView::hidden`) is in ‰ of the length left above the lift, up to 1000; the caller passes 0 while the player has it off. With `L` the lift's length, its share is `K = (lane length − L)·hidden/1000` (`play/LaneRenderer.java:335-345`). It is an overlay: the notes under it are drawn and the scroll span does not change. The lanes paint none of it ("Cover paint").
  - **Cover paint.** What SUDDEN+, LIFT and HIDDEN+ paint is ordinary elements (`Source::CoverPaint`, a `CoverPaintDef` in `WidgetDefs::cover_paints`): an image, or an image choice table's entry on that entry's track and native size (R6), drawn in the element's place in the draw order with its own track, timer, visibility, blend, clip and placement. It moves by its `Follow`, by the cover it paints (`CoverMotion`: none, `Lift` up by `L`, or `Hidden` up by `L + K`, from the frame's one lane geometry, so a `Follow::Cover` adds SUDDEN+'s slide once) and, as a slider (`slider_range`, `R` reference pixels up to `MAX_COORD`), down by `R · cover · (1000 − lift) / 10⁶` of the frame's effective amounts, an exact integer numerator divided once in `f64`; 0 while SUDDEN+ is off. Edges keep their fractions until drawn. A `Hidden` paint draws only while HIDDEN+ is on (`CoverSettings::hidden_enabled`), at any amount, 0 included.
  - **Trim.** A trim line (`CoverTrim`: none, `Fixed(t)`, or `Lift(b)` at `t = b − L`, y down) gates a paint against its gate rect `[x, y, w, h]`: its `gate_track`, the unstretched destination sampled on the element's timer and moved as the paint is, or else the paint's own sampled rect, before any native size or rotation (`play/SkinHidden.java:61-90`, `skin/SkinObject.java:649-667`). With `y ≥ t` it draws nothing; with `y < t < y + h` it draws under the clip `[x, y, w, t − y]`, mapped through the binding and the region's transform but never rotated, and nothing when that clip has no area (`skin/Skin.java:567`); otherwise it draws unclipped, so a fitted image whose destination lies wholly above the line overhangs it. The quad keeps its whole uv, pivot and rotation: the clip is a `ClipRect` of finite positive area carried by the batch's run (`QuadBatch::push_clipped`, `Run::clip_rect`), merged only with a neighbour of the same key and clip, which the renderer intersects with the element's own clip (§6, "Render integration"). A hidden gate track draws nothing.
  - **Paint and coverage.** A paint never changes the coverage, the scroll span or the notes: a hidden, transparent, timer-off or empty paint draws nothing and nothing replaces it, so the lanes with all three covers may still hide the whole lane. Bounds and the variant use the paint's authored tracks; its gate, trim and motion add nothing. `Skin::new` checks every definition, referenced or not, refusing (`LoadError::CoverPaint`, naming the paint) an unknown image or table, a slider range past `MAX_COORD`, a slider with a motion, a trim or a gate (`SliderMotion`), a gate without a trim, a gate outside the coordinates or of negative size, and a trim base past `MAX_COORD`; then a trimmed element without a gate whose own or choice tracks have a negative size (`LoadError::CoverGate`), and a moving paint that follows the lift (`UnsupportedFollow`), since its motion already holds it. A paint's slider extends the coordinate check downward by `R`, a moving paint's tracks and gate are checked moved by a whole lane length as a follower's, and a linked trim base lowered by it must stay within `MAX_COORD`. A gate's keys count toward `MAX_SKIN_KEYS` once.
  - **Followers.** An image, number, gauge or text element (bitmap text included) and a judgment display's grade part carry a `Follow`: none, `Lift`, `Cover` or `LiftAndCover`. With `L` the lift's length and `C` SUDDEN+'s slide this frame (R3), it moves by `−L`, `+C` or `C − L` reference pixels, and by 0 in a skin without lanes. `Follow::translate` moves the sampled rect's y in `f64`, keeping x, w and h, before the lane map and the region transform (R5), so the pivot moves with the rect, a region's scale scales the motion, and tracks, clocks, clips and run order are unchanged; a text's raster request does not change. The combo has no `Follow`: it attaches to its grade's translated rect. The lanes and the judgment display as a whole refuse a `Follow` (`LoadError::UnsupportedFollow`), and `Skin::new` refuses a follower whose authored keys, moved by a whole lane length (`ceil` of the source height) either way, leave ±`MAX_COORD`.
  - **Notes leave** on the authored lanes when judged (a tap) or at their time or when hit (a mine); a long note when its end passes below the line.
  - **An imported skin's notes** draw as its source does (`play/LaneRenderer.java:546-581, 701-727`), which draws nothing below the line: a note sits on its time by its lower edge, `offset y` above the lanes' bottom, its geometry kept unrounded; a tap or mine stays until its time, judged or not; a long note stays while its end is ahead (`display < end`, its end above its head or the line), its body from the end's lower edge to the head's upper edge, and its end cap is drawn only when the end is judged on its own (CN or HCN: the chart's mode, else `ln_default`).
  - **Bar line groups.** The lanes hold a table of groups (`LanesDef::bar_groups`, a `BarGroupDef` each, at most `MAX_BAR_GROUPS`, 16 384), each an image with its own track, timer, visibility, blend and placement (pivot, binding, native size and `Follow`) in the lanes' region, with no fill; its colour multiplies the lanes' tint and the lanes' clip still applies. At each chart bar line at position `p` a group's sampled rect, moved by its `Follow`, moves by `−(p − at_line) · px_per_pos` of the frame's scale, so at the display head it stands where its track places it, whatever the notes' height or `offset y`. It draws its whole quad, uncropped, when its vertical extent after the region's transform overlaps the lanes' band by a positive height; touching the band draws nothing, and with no scroll span no bar draws. Bars draw before the line and the notes, in chart bar order and then group order, through a min-heap of the groups' cursors, each cursor over the bars a conservative window holds (the bounds inverted through the scale, widened by `256 · f32::EPSILON · M` for the construction's rounding, `M` the largest magnitude involved, then rounded outward). Each group is sampled once a frame, its visibility evaluated once; a hidden, timer-off or empty group visits nothing. A `Play` holds a frame record and a heap slot per group, made in `prepare` only when the skin has groups and lent to the lanes for each `build`. `Skin::new` refuses more groups than the cap before building any, and a group with an unknown timer, a non-`bool` visibility, coordinates or a `Follow` reach past `MAX_COORD`, another region or a fill (`LanesError::BarGroup`, naming the group); a group's keys count toward `MAX_SKIN_KEYS` and its visibility toward `MAX_SKIN_OPS`.
  - **Judgment-time placement** (a tracked gap, not an accepted difference): a note is drawn at its exact beat position with the frame's playhead, so at its integer-µs judgment time it can be off the line by one µs of scroll plus the scroll map's Q80 quantization (design.md "Chart Time"). That is sub-pixel at ordinary BPMs, though D1 rounding can move a drawn edge by one pixel. The exact placement and what it needs are https://github.com/shuymn/tapestry/issues/167, closed by the last S6c PR (§6).
  - **Section targets.** In a practice round only the section's targets are drawn (notes from its start, ending before its end, as the evaluator decides), and once evaluation is complete the round rings out without notes (`SkinFrame::complete`).
  - **Budget.** `LANE_QUADS` (8 192) quads for notes and bar lines plus 1 reserved for the line; every cover paint is an image quad of its own, emitted whether or not the notes ran out, and its gate adds none (followers take no reservation of their own), and `LANE_VISITS` (16 384) candidates visited, charged before any skip (judged, outside the section, culled); a bar line costs a visit per group that considers it and a quad per group that draws it, from the same counters, so a skin's groups never multiply the budget; running out stops the frame's lanes with one drop.
  - **Cropping.** A part crossing the lanes' top or bottom is cut with its uv, never squashed; a bar line draws whole ("Bar line groups").
  - The lanes' track, like the judgment display's, gives its color and alpha only; geometry or an angle is a load error. Number and gauge elements are placed by their track like images.
  - The scroll math is today's `layout::Scale` (crates/render/src/simple.rs:60-115), copied into `skin-model` parameterized by the span and the line, until render switches over and then deleted from render; with no cover or lift every output bit equals render's, and a test reads render's source to guard the copy. Render's own lanes draw no cover or lift yet; they take the model's (`LaneStyle::Simple`) when render switches over.
- `number` (S5b): a glyph strip (`DigitsDef`) showing an `int` expression over the play schema, charged to the op budget once per element that draws it.
  - **Glyphs.** `Ten` (digits), `Eleven` (a padding glyph 10) or `TwentyFour` (12 glyphs for values at or above 0, then 12 for values below, each half with the padding glyph 10 and a sign 11). An image of `k` times as many frames holds `k` glyph sets, cycled over the sets on the image's own timer; set 0 shows while that timer is off.
  - **Cells.** 1 to `MAX_CELLS` (11: the 10 digits of an `i32` and a sign). Cells fill from the right with the digits of `|v|` (`unsigned_abs`, so `i32::MIN` shows every digit); a value longer than the strip keeps its low digits. Left of the digits, the padding: zero, glyph 10, or blank. With `TwentyFour`, zero or glyph padding gives cell 0 to the sign, and blank padding puts the sign in the first blank left of the digits, so a value filling every cell shows no sign; without `TwentyFour` a negative value shows its magnitude.
  - **Geometry.** The track's rect is cell 0; cell `j` is at `x + p·j − s` with the pitch `p = w + space` (space signed, within ±2^20) and `s` 0, `p·b` or `p·b/2` for right, left or centre alignment over `b` blank cells. Each cell turns about its own 9-point pivot. A bound number maps each cell's x and w through the lane map, so the spacing follows the lane width. Its logical box is, per key, cell 0's rect with cell `n − 1`'s.
  - **Digit offsets** (S6c-09). `DigitsDef::offsets` holds at most one `DigitOffset { dx, dy, dw, dh }` per cell, in target pixels with y down, each component within ±2^20; offset `j` belongs to fixed cell `j` (cell 0 the leftmost) whatever the alignment, padding, sign or blanks, and a cell past the table has none. At placement (`Brush::quad_offset`) the cell's rect is placed and mapped to the canvas as without one, the offset divided by the canvas's scale is added in `f64`, and only then is a native size (stretch 8 and 9) fitted into the moved rect and the 9-point pivot taken from it, so the delta is the same at every target size and overlapping cells keep their cell order. A zero offset is none. The logical box, the region, the variant and a combo's shift keep the unmoved cells: an offset moves paint only, and the logical box may not cover it. `Skin::new` checks each strip an element draws, as it checks the strip's other parameters (more offsets than cells is `NumberError::OffsetCount`, a component outside ±2^20 `OffsetRange`); a strip no element draws is not checked and never drawn. It counts the offsets of every number and every judge combo it defines, drawn or not, against `MAX_DIGIT_OFFSETS` (65 536), `LoadError::TooManyDigitOffsets` past it, before anything is loaded; `Skin::digit_offset_count` gives the count. A loaded strip owns its offsets, so `DigitsDef` and `Digits` are no longer `Copy`.
- `judge` (S5b): one grade image per slot (`JudgeSlot`: PERFECT, GREAT, GOOD, BAD, POOR, empty POOR, PERFECT at a full gauge) and one optional combo strip per slot, each part with its own track, timer, 9-point pivot, blend and visibility.
  - **What it shows.** The reducer's latest judgment's slot, nothing before the first one. PERFECT while `gauge_max` is on uses the full-gauge slot; its grade image and its combo each fall back to PERFECT's on their own. The combo shows the snapshot's `combo` field, only when it is above 0, the slot has a combo, and the combo part's timer is on and its visibility true; it reads no value from the effect, which may be behind the snapshot (R3). Nothing is drawn while the grade part is not.
  - **Placement.** The combo's track is relative to the grade's sampled rect, after the grade's follow translation: the combo has no `Follow` of its own and moves with its grade: x from its left edge, y from its top or bottom edge (`Attach`). With `shift`, the grade moves left by half the combo's drawn width `p·(cells − blanks)`, its digit offsets ignored. The combo's cells are pushed first, then the grade, each under its part's blend and the element's clip, so a judgment display is at most two runs. The element's own track multiplies its color and alpha into both parts.
  - **Region.** In `judge` (in `screen` under `Fit::Fixed`) and never bound to the lanes. Its logical box is the union over the effective states (each slot, and the full-gauge PERFECT with its fallbacks) of the grade's keys and the combo's strip boxes moved over the grade corner's range on the attach edge, with the grade's box widened on both sides by half the widest strip when `shift` is on. Every part's keys count toward the skin's key limit, and part visibilities are charged per element.
- `gauge` (S5b): the shown gauge (`SkinFrame::gauge`) as segments of the track's rect.
  - **Looks.** Front, back and tip images, each at or above and below the border, for each look (`GaugeLook`: assist easy, easy, normal, hard, ex hard, hazard). The descriptor's look comes from the preset gauge's id, or for another id from its kind (an end-threshold gauge is normal, a survival gauge hard); the schema's `gauge.kind` (R3) and the importer's gauge-type conditions read that same look, so no other classification exists. Its border is the clear threshold or the value a survival gauge fails below.
  - **Segments.** `P` segments (1 to `MAX_SEGMENTS`, 1 000). With the value `V` and the maximum `M`, `n = 0` lit segments when `V ≤ 0`, else `⌊V·P/M⌋` within 1 to `P`, so any non-zero gauge lights one; segment `i` (from 1) is below the border when `i·M < border·P`. `MAX_SEGMENTS` is a policy cap, one segment per ‰: the widget reads the gauge's exact value, not the ‰ field.
  - **Motions,** pure functions of the element's timer, with `k = ⌊t / step⌋` and the offset `a`: still (`a = 0`), random (`a` = splitmix64 of `k` mod `range + 1`), rise (`a = −k mod (range + 1)`), fall (`a = k mod (range + 1)`). Segment `n` is the tip, below it front while `i + a < n`, the rest back. Flicker draws front up to `n` and back after it, and overlays the tip on segment `n` with alpha `1 − |2φ − T|/T` at the phase `φ` of the period `T`. These differ from the target: its flicker curve differs (1.032 against 0.970 at 16 ms of a 33 ms period), though its phase follows elapsed time modulo the period as here; its rise, fall and random take at most one step per prepare call once the step's time has passed, drawn or not, then schedule the next a step after that time (`play/SkinGauge.java:110`), rather than following elapsed time, so their phase and catch-up after a frame gap differ; and its random sequence differs.
  - Each segment turns about its own 9-point pivot, and a bound gauge maps x and w through the lane map.
- `text` (S5c): one line (`TextDef`) in a font file of the sheet (`FontRef::File`) or the player's text font (`FontRef::Default`: the menu font in the player, the test font in the preview and goldens).
  - **Strings.** A literal of 1 to 256 bytes with no control character (C0, DEL or C1), or a `TextField`: title, subtitle, artist, subartist, genre, and the full title and full artist, joined with a space when the second part is not empty (refs 12 and 16, the most used). The caller passes `ChartText` filled from one record, the chart's static top-level header (what `bms_parser::read_header` reads and the library stores, as the select screen shows it), for display only. Headers inside `#IF`, `#SWITCH` or `#SETSWITCH` blocks are ignored, overrides of a top-level value included, and a field with no top-level declaration is empty. Table, target, rival and player names do not exist in tapestry; string expressions and first-non-empty fallbacks are not in the model.
  - **Requests.** `Skin::text_requests(&ChartText, target, &Layout)` runs at `prepare` and may allocate. Per text element (its slot is its place among the text elements in draw order) whose visibility does not fold to false: the string, control characters dropped after the full fields are joined, cut to 256 bytes at a character boundary; an empty one gives no request. The em is `min(largest key h × canvas scale × the region's effective scale, 256)` pixels, from the exact fractions `variant` uses (`Fit::Fixed` ignores layout adjustments). An element in a hidden region still asks, since the adjust mode can show it.
  - **Table.** The rasterizer (S8) answers a request with a `TextImage`: its uv and layer, the ink rect and the pen advance, in em. `TextImage::new` derives the line's extent, `left = min(0, ink.x)` and `width = max(advance, ink.x + ink.w) − left`, and refuses a non-finite value, ink 0 wide or tall, a negative advance, and any value or extent past ±4 096 em. `Skin::attach_text` checks the table's length against the text slots and gives it to the `Play`, which every `prepare` makes without one, so a table never outlives its attempt.
  - **Layout.** The track's rect `[x, y, w, h]` is the box: `h` is the em, and the box's top is the font's cap height, which the rasterizer folds into the ink rect. With `tw = width·h`, `Shrink` squeezes horizontally to `sx = w / tw` only when `tw > w`, else `sx = 1`, so it never enlarges; the drawn width `d = tw·sx` sits at offset 0, `(w − d)/2` or `w − d` for left, centre and right, and `Spill` overflows (centred text both ways). The quad is `[x + off + (ink.x − left)·h·sx, y + ink.y·h, ink.w·h·sx, ink.h·h]`, turned about the element's 9-point pivot on the box, sampled linearly, with the track's color and alpha over white premultiplied coverage. A line drawn 0 wide, a slot without an image and a table of another length draw nothing. The metrics are in em, so the raster size changes only sharpness, and an animated `h` rescales the line uniformly. The text stays as prepared through a resize (R5).
  - **Bitmap layout** (`FontRef::Bitmap`, S6b-3). `Skin::attach_bitmap_text(&mut Play, &ChartText)` runs at `prepare` beside `attach_text`, chooses and cleans the string as the requests do, and lays out each bitmap slot whose visibility does not fold to false as the source's runtime lays out one run without tight bounds (gdx 1.9.9 `GlyphLayout`), in em of the font's `size`: the pen starts at 0 and moves by each glyph's advance plus the kerning to the next, applied only when the previous glyph is the primary's own basic-plane glyph and the next is in the basic plane; the line's width is the last pen plus its advance. A glyph's ink is `[pen + xoffset, yoffset − yAdj − ascent, width, height] / size` from the box's top, with `ascent = base − (capHeight − padTop − padBottom)` and, for a glyph from a fallback, `yAdj = fallback.rep − primary.rep`, `rep` being the raw `yoffset` of the first of `S P A N O T H E R [ ] M 0` the font has (else its first char line with a height). A character missing from the primary comes from the fallbacks in order, then (basic plane) the wave dash and fullwidth tilde stand in for each other, then `□ ▢ ☐ ■ ?` in the primary and each fallback, then (basic plane) the primary's missing glyph (`char id=0` or `-1`), else it is skipped; a substitute carries no kerning. A glyph 0 wide or tall advances but draws nothing, and a line with a value past ±4 096 em gets none. Each frame `Text::draw_glyphs` places the line as the outline text's layout does, with `width` the line's (a line of width ≤ 0 still draws, never squeezed), one quad per glyph `[x + off + ink.x·h·sx, y + ink.y·h, ink.w·h·sx, ink.h·h]`, its uv found inside its page's frame in the atlas that frame (`(u1 − u0)/page width` is exactly one texel), sampled linearly; a page frame missing from the uvs draws nothing. A fresh `prepare` has no bitmap lines.
  - **Load checks** in `Skin::new`'s order: more than 64 text elements is `TooManyTexts`; `UnknownWidget` covers a missing `TextDef`; a font index past the sheet's fonts is `UnknownFont`; a text element bound to the lanes is `Bound`, one with a fill `Fill`, and one with a key of `h ≤ 0` or `w < 0` `Size`; a bad literal is `Literal`. Text charges no ops beyond its visibility. Its region box is its keys' rects: spilled ink does not count. `Skin::variant` skips text, which has its own pixel size.
  - **Fonts.** `Sheet::with_fonts` takes at most 16 font files, each name at most 1 024 bytes, resolved inside the skin folder to a regular file of at most 32 MiB, 64 MiB in all, whose first 4 bytes are `00 01 00 00`, `OTTO`, `true` or `ttcf`; `skin-model` parses none of them. Bitmap `.fnt` fonts (BMFont text) come through `SheetBuilder::admit_fnt(name, charge)`: in order, a font slot (outline fonts and distinct `.fnt` files share the 16), one file attempt, the name and confinement checks, a regular file of at most 4 MiB, its length charged to the 64 MiB and never refunded, the read, then `FntFile::parse` with the per-font caps (16 384 glyphs and 16 384 kerning pairs, a synthesised space counted) or the sheet's remaining 65 536 of each if fewer; then each page in turn charged its attempt, bytes, image and frame and admitted as a whole-file image of one linear frame, all pages or none, each glyph's rectangle inside its page, and each page decoded once and dropped, so a corrupt page fails its font rather than the atlas. Every charge stays spent when the admission fails. Two counters bound the bytes, each at 512 MiB a variant: the importer's ledger counts every font page charged, failed admissions included, and so bounds the page reading across admissions; the builder's totals count only the files the sheet keeps, ordinary images and admitted pages, and so bound what the atlas reads. A page of a failed admission counts only in the ledger, so failed pages and kept ordinary files together can reach about 1 GiB, while font pages stay within 512 MiB. `charge` is called before each guarded step and its refusal aborts. `add_bitmap_font(primary, fallbacks)` forms a chain (at most 15 fallbacks, 64 chains) that `FontRef::Bitmap` names. A sheet holding bitmap fonts takes no fonts through `with_fonts` (`FontError::BitmapFonts`): its outline fonts are admitted with `admit_font`, so both kinds share the slots and bytes. The parser is strict: every byte UTF-8 but the quoted value of line 1's own `face` attribute, each line's encoding checked before its fields, and a carriage return allowed only before a line feed; line 1 is `info` with `size` and a `padding` of four integers; line 2 `common` with `lineHeight`, `base`, `scaleW`, `scaleH`, `pages` at their positions (pages 1 to 16); then the page lines `id file` with ids in order; then the `char` lines `id x y width height xoffset yoffset xadvance page` (later keys ignored), ids `-1` or `0` the missing glyph, 1 to 0x10FFFF glyphs, no duplicate; then the `kerning` lines `first second amount`, amounts −128 to 127, only blank lines after them. Values are at most 16 384, `chars count` is declared at most once, a declared `count` must match the lines read, and a refused file is a file-local `FntError`. As the source stores them, a duplicate kerning pair's last wins, a pair whose first glyph is absent is dropped, and a missing space takes the advance of `l` or the first glyph drawn with a size. A font with no basic-plane glyph drawn with a size (the missing glyph aside) is refused.
  - Not in the model: wrapping, truncation, outline, shadow and editable text; the surveyed play skins use none of them.
- **Budgets.** At most `cells` quads for a number, `segments` (one more with flicker) for a gauge, the most over a judgment display's effective states of 1 plus the resolved combo's cells, and 1 for a text, or for one in a bitmap font its literal's characters or 256 for a field. The skin's widget quads are capped at `MAX_WIDGET_QUADS` (16 384, the element limit). `quad_capacity` adds them to the images' and the lanes', so the worst batch is 40 961 entries (16 384 image quads, cover paints among them, 16 384 widget quads, `LANE_QUADS` and the line's reservation), at `64 + size_of::<Run>()` B an entry: 2 621 504 B of quads (64 B each) and 1 474 596 B of runs (36 B each, an inline clip among them), 4 096 100 B without container overhead.

### R7. Images and scaling

Before play, never per frame, so per-frame cost is unchanged apart from trilinear sampling. Loading a skin (`Sheet::new`) checks its files and images and reads only their headers; the atlas (`Sheet::atlas`) decodes, packs and builds the mips for the chosen variant whenever that variant changes, between plays and off the in-play path, so a retry keeps it. This is how the loading thread and `prepare`'s pick of @2x (R5) fit together. A file whose dimensions changed since load is refused, and the player keeps its current skin. A sheet also holds generated images of one colour (`SolidImageDef`, `SheetBuilder::add_solid_image`), such as the importer's fallback note paint: one frame, never animated, at one size in both variants; each charges an image, a frame and both variants' texel totals but no file, attempt or byte, its cells sort after the files', and the atlas writes its premultiplied colour through the whole allocation, gutter included, after the files are read, reading nothing for it.
- **Atlas pages** packed with `etagere`, sized within the limits the device was created with (`Device::limits()`; device creation requests defaults today, crates/render/src/gpu.rs:95), not the adapter's, so a skin that fits the adapter but not the device is refused at load rather than failing at texture creation. The pages are one texture array: every layer has one power-of-two side, from 256 (or the largest frame) to the device's side capped at 8 192, chosen as the fewest texels across all layers, reserved ones included, among the sides whose pages fit the device's layers and 64. The skin's pages start after the reserved layers (the bitmap font's layer 0), and a frame's uv is its content over the side, exact in `f32`.
- **Mipmaps** generated on the CPU, 4 levels, with premultiplied alpha (`(c·a + 127) / 255`) and a 2×2 box filter in stored (non-sRGB) space. Every frame of a grid and every glyph of a digit strip is packed on its own with an 8-texel gutter, its allocation aligned to 8, so every texel of every level covers one allocation and lower levels never mix neighbouring frames. The gutter and any rounding in the allocation repeat the frame's nearest edge texel, as clamp-to-edge would. Two images cutting the same rectangle share one packed frame. The tests use contrasting adjacent frames and colored transparent edges and sample them at every level.
- **Per-image filter:** linear by default, nearest for pixel art: nearest within a level and between levels, so shrinking still uses the box-filtered levels and never blurs two levels together.
- **Text** (S8; uploaded in S10) rasterized at `prepare` at its final pixel size: `cosmic-text` lays the line out and swash renders white premultiplied coverage, with the features `std` and `swash` and fonts passed as bytes, never memory-mapped or found through fontconfig; a bounded `Sheet::font_data(i)` reads them and checks the path again, as atlas builds do. A glyph the skin's font lacks comes from the default font, then its `.notdef`. The play screen still never calls into `render::text`.
  - **Collections.** Only a validated face 0 is registered (as the target's FreeType generator uses face 0). No bulk loader that enumerates or reserves by a collection's declared face count is called (fontdb 0.23's `load_font_data` reserves `count` IDs before validating); if a bulk API is used, the `ttcf` face count is read and bounded first. A 12-byte `ttcf` header with count `0xffffffff` is refused by a test.
  - **Cap height,** the box's top: a positive `sCapHeight`, else the positive top above the baseline of the same face's own `H` glyph (no glyph fallback), else a positive ascender; zero and absent values both fall through. Tests cover a zero and an absent `sCapHeight`.
  - **Text layer.** The lines are packed into one layer after the skin's pages, reserved in `Sheet::atlas`'s layer count and texel budget when the skin has text, its side at least 2 048, with `etagere`, the existing gutter, alignment and mips. A line is at most `min(4096, side − 2·GUTTER)` pixels wide; a wider one is set at a smaller em. When the layer is full, every request is set again at half the em cap, at most 8 rounds.
  - **Raster bounds.** Each image's pixel dimensions and temporary raster bytes are bounded before anything is allocated, since a font's units per em and glyph bounds can make one glyph thousands of em tall. A request that still does not fit after the refit rounds gives no image for its slot (nothing drawn, one report line), not an error for the whole `prepare`.
- **@2x variants** (S4): a file may name an @2x file at exactly twice its size. `Skin::variant` picks them when some image the skin can draw is magnified on both axes: per element the canvas scale times its region's scale (hidden regions skipped), a `cover` or `stretch` fill's own scale on `screen`, and for lane-bound images and the lanes `min(lane width, 100 %)` on x, so a lane width alone never picks @2x; a bar group (R6) counts with the lanes, under their region and visibility, by its own binding, so an unbound group takes no lane width, and a group whose visibility folds to false does not count; exact rationals, one variant for the sheet. The atlas then doubles the frames' rectangles; a file without one keeps its 1x frames, and track geometry does not change. A scale change in the adjust mode reaches the assets at the next `prepare`. The authored syntax for @2x, and nine-slice, come with the authored format (S12).
- **Elements and quads** (S4): an element's source is an image of the sheet, or one chosen from a table (R6), each entry fitting its own native size under stretch 8 and 9; `Skin::build` takes the uv table of an atlas of the skin's sheet (a table of another sheet draws nothing) and writes the frame's uv and layer and the image's filter into the `Quad`. Neither breaks a run: blend and clip alone do (R1).
- **Bounds:** file, image and frame counts, name length, file size, header position, image dimensions and texels per file and per variant, atlas texels, and element, keyframe and timer counts are capped before anything that grows with them is allocated, and one file's decoding is bounded (design.md "Memory Budget"). Every referenced file resolves inside the skin's folder and must be a regular file, checked before opening and on the handle (design.md threat model "Skins", with its precondition and the decoder exception).

## 4. The authored format (`skin-toml`)

**Concepts an author learns,** each with its payoff:

| Concept | What it gives the author |
| --- | --- |
| image | A file with optional grid frames, a frame cycle and its own timer (R2), as authors make sprite sheets today |
| element | One placement model for images, numbers, text, bars and widgets, in reference pixels |
| region | 21:9 support and player adjustment, by tagging HUD elements (the playfield and judgment are automatic) |
| expression | Conditions and values by name (`combo >= 100`) instead of numeric IDs |
| timer | What starts an animation: a built-in event or `when(<condition>)` |
| keyframes | Animation, with the semantics of R2 |
| option | Variants the player picks, folded at load |

`per_lane = true` repeats an element over the lanes with the lane's x and width; it is an attribute, not a concept. Cut: generic templates, includes, file choosers, free offsets, expressions that move or color things, subtract and multiply blends.

**Files.** `skin.toml` (TOML 1.1 through `toml` with `serde_spanned`) beside the images. `[meta]` (name, author, version, format version), `[options.*]`, `[images.*]`, then elements in draw order. A file of another format version is refused (AGENTS.md). Errors carry spans and are rendered with `annotate-snippets`: unknown names with a suggestion, type and range errors, and export warnings. KDL reads better for trees but has no maintained derive layer; YAML's implicit typing hurts hand authors.

**How the export always works:**
1. A construct enters the format only together with its export rule.
2. Expressions decide only visibility, numbers, text, frame index and bar ratio; geometry and color come from keyframes and regions, because beatoraja's keyframe geometry is plain integers and takes no Lua.
3. Expressions stay in a Lua-translatable subset (R4); division, remainder and conditionals become prelude functions (a conditional because `c and a or b` breaks when `a` is false).
4. Every state field states its beatoraja mapping or is tapestry-only. A decorative element that reads a tapestry-only field is left out of the export and listed in the report; `--strict` fails instead. The elements the play depends on (the lanes, judgment and gauge widgets, and the score and combo numbers) may depend only on exportable fields, in every option configuration: `check` refuses a skin that breaks this, so `build` never writes an export without notes. A fixture tests the refusal.

## 5. The beatoraja exporter (`skin-export`) and the CLI (`skin-cli`)

- **Target:** a generated `.luaskin` at exactly 1920×1080 (a test asserts the size: an unlisted size silently falls back to 1280×720 in both loaders), using only the fields and runtime API that upstream and ED share. Conditions are `draw` Lua strings, because ED's `op` takes integers only. The loaders compile each such string on its own in the skin's global environment, so the prelude helpers are installed as globals with a reserved prefix, not as locals of the generated file. A flag for upstream-only fields can come later. JSON was rejected: it cannot compute geometry and expands long.
- **Regions** become one user offset per region. The Lua reads the configured values at load (ED exports them before the configured pass, `LuaSkinLoader`, `SkinLuaAccessor`) and bakes each region's transform into its elements' keyframes; no destination offset applies them a second time. The built-in offsets are used only where their meaning matches: lift and lane cover for the covers. The whole-skin offset scales the lanes and BGA too, so it does not stand in for a HUD region.
- **Text:** literal text is baked into images at 1x. Chart-bound text exports as `text` plus `font`, copying the author's font file, which must be redistributable and cover the needed glyphs: `size` is the largest key `h`, `Shrink` is `overflow` 1 and `Spill` 0, and the destination x moves right by `w·{0, ½, 1}` for left, centre and right. Chart-bound text in the default font cannot ship a redistributable font, so it is left out with a report line. A title cannot be baked into a reusable skin.
- **Judgments and gauges** map by a table: tapestry's grades, empty POOR and the preset's gauges to beatoraja's grades and gauge types; a gauge with no counterpart maps to the nearest type with a report line. A `gauge.kind` comparison is remapped explicitly onto the target's gauge types, never exported as the enum's storage, and one the target cannot express is reported.
- **Score rates** export by recomputing the guarded integer formula of R3 from the target's own score and note counts, never by rebuilding basis points from its rounded rate properties (102 and 103, 115 and 116); a rate expression the target cannot compute that way is reported.
- **Widgets** export as the target's `value`, `judge` and `gauge`, and what differs is reported:
  - Numbers: a `Ten` image whose frame count is a multiple of 24 is rewritten, since the target tries 24 first: a cycled strip is split, keeping its set boundaries and wrap, and padding is allowed only for one set or no cycle. `Eleven` with blank or zero padding is reported (the target forces glyph padding) or rewritten to `Ten`. The target hides `i32::MIN` and `i32::MAX`, which is reported.
  - Judgment displays: a `TwentyFour` combo is not exportable (the target's judge takes 10 or 11 glyphs) and is reported. A `Bottom` combo exports directly; a `Top` combo exports only where, per effective state (each slot and the full-gauge PERFECT, grade and combo resolved), every grade key has one height, giving `combo y = grade h − cy − combo h` per key; when a fallback-shared `Top` combo needs different offsets in different states, separate combo entries are written (grade 0 of height 80 and grade 6 of height 100 sharing a `Top` combo at y 10, h 20 give offsets 50 and 70). Otherwise it is reported as not representable.
  - Gauges: a part with an animated image or a non-zero angle is reported (or baked to frame 0 and angle 0 with a report line). Each motion exports as the target type of its name, `Still` as random with range 0, and the report states the motion differences (R6).
- **What degrades:** aspect (16:9 only), the side inset, mipmaps and @2x (the 1x image), nine-slice (baked per size, edges rounded so seams do not open).
- **CLI `tapestry-skin`:** `check`; `preview`, which draws the compiled quads with `tiny-skia` into PNGs at 16:9 and 21:9 for a fixture state, so authors preview on macOS and Linux; `export beatoraja`; `build`, which does all three in one command; `watch`, which polls file times as preset files are polled (stage2.md D10). The preview lives in the CLI, so the player does not link it.
- **In the player** a changed skin is reloaded between plays, built off-thread; a failed reload keeps the current skin, and nothing is polled during play.

### Render integration

- `SpriteBatch` is one ordered list with runs broken at each blend or clip change, drawn in order with a pipeline per blend (S10-01) and each run under its clip's scissor (S10-02). The clips are the skin's fixed table (`render::ClipTable`), installed with its atlas; their scissors are projected once per target size (design.md "Rendering and Skins", D1), an unclipped run sets the whole target back, and a clip that keeps no pixel draws nothing. A `QuadBatch` run may also carry an inline clip (`Run::clip_rect`, a cover paint's trim, R6) in canvas pixels: render consumes it in S10-05 (pending), drawing the run under the intersection of its fixed clip and that rectangle, projected once with D1's rounding and clamped to the viewport, an empty intersection drawing nothing. Imported destination `clip_*` fields stay outside this.
- A sprite is staged into a 64-byte `Instance`, converted field by field: rect, uv, colour, the quad's pivot and angle, turned in the vertex shader, and the atlas layer and filter packed into flags (S10-01). The atlas is a texture array of one page side (S10-02), the bitmap font at the top left of reserved layer 0, always sampled at its nearest level-0 texel with its uvs scaled at staging by 128 over the page side, and the skin's pages after it (R7), uploaded with their mips as `Rgba8Unorm`, with a linear and a nearest sampler chosen per quad. `Renderer::atlas_limits` gives `AtlasLimits` from `Device::limits()`; `Renderer::install_atlas` checks the pages before allocating and keeps the atlas and clips in use when it refuses them. Planned for S10-03: the uv table and the uploaded pages have one owner, and the memory budget counts the old atlas alive during a reload and the upload staging.
- Premultiplied alpha throughout the sprite pipelines, the bitmap font and `text_screen` included (S10-01): staging multiplies a tint's RGB by its alpha once; alpha blending is RGB 1, 1 − source alpha, additive RGB 1, 1, and the alpha channel composites over in both. Menu text keeps its straight colour (`TextStyle.color`), blended by glyphon.
- The canvas projection replaces the letterbox for play frames (S10-02): `PlaySkin::canvas` places the skin's canvas on the target as the viewport, menus and results keep the fixed letterboxed canvas, and `FrameInput::target` carries the size the renderer applied. The window's size reaches the render thread through a slot of the newest size, applied between frames by `Renderer::resize`; a minimized window presents nothing until a nonzero size comes back (design.md "Rendering and Skins").
- The screen's recovery and an attempt's preparation are separate (S10-03a, design.md "Play Lifecycle", preparing each attempt). `RenderMsg::Prepare` builds a failed screen again, without a chart, and prepares nothing; `RenderMsg::PrepareAttempt` carries the owned `AttemptContext`, from which the render thread makes the borrowed `PlayContext` for the one call `Screen::prepare_attempt`, and answers `Preparation::Ready`, `Preparation::Refused` (a skin's refusal while the device is healthy: the screen is kept and no failure is reported) or an error (the device or the surface: the screen is dropped, and the next recovery builds it again). The game thread waits for the answer in 10 ms slices within the render answer bound, and a quit key, a close or a failure of the input or audio thread stops the wait, the failure then ending the run with its cause. `RenderMsg::EndLoading` answers whether the screen is still there, and `ScreenError::Lost` refuses the opening when a loading frame dropped it after the preparation; the game also checks the input thread, the audio output and the screen just before recording opens. Each loading page travels with its `LoadingUpdate` in the same slot and loading lifetime.
- One batch for every play skin (S10-03b-1): `SpriteBatch` is the runtime model's `QuadBatch`, which `SimpleSkin` pushes into and a model skin writes into directly, with nothing copied through per-blend lists; after the skin's sprites the staging appends the measurement overlay from a reserve of 1 024 sprites in one run that the skin cannot fill. `PlaySkin::build`, `Renderer::build` and `Screen::build` return `Result<(), skin_model::BuildError>`: a refused frame submits nothing, the renderer builds that attempt no more until the next attempt's preparation, and the render thread stops the play as a screen failure while keeping the screen, its atlas, clips and device for the next preparation; device and surface failures keep the screen-dropping recovery (design.md "Rendering and Skins", refused frames).
- The declarative skin on the play screen (S10-03b-2, design.md "Rendering and Skins", declarative skin installation): `PlaySkin::prepare` takes the attempt (`render::AttemptInput`: the chart, the borrowed `PlayContext`, the five chart labels and the applied target) and `PlaySkin::load_chart` a chart's load, which prepares nothing preset-dependent. `render::SkinBundle` is the immutable CPU description of an installation, checked when made against its skin's frames, fixed clips and the renderer's reserved layers (`render::BundleError`), and `render::DeclSkin` the `PlaySkin` drawing it, a fresh `Play` per attempt with its bitmap lines and the text an `AttemptText` producer prepares; `render::skin_frame` is the frame adapter. `Renderer::stage` uploads and validates a bundle's atlas, clips, staging and instance buffer beside the installation in use and prepares the fresh attempt before it returns a `PreparedToken` (request and device generation); `Renderer::commit` swaps them together and refuses another request's or device's token; `Renderer::discard` and a refusal drop them whole; `Renderer::restore` installs a committed bundle on a renderer built again; `Renderer::memory` witnesses the peak, both attempts' state included. A device refusal of the staged uploads or instance buffer is a refusal inside one error scope. On the render thread, `RenderMsg::PrepareAttempt` carries the link's request number and is answered between frames (`Screen::prepare_attempt` returns `None` until it answers), `RenderMsg::Commit` and `RenderMsg::Discard` finish or drop it (`RenderLink::commit_attempt`, `discard_attempt`, behind `Rig::commit_attempt` and `Rig::discard_attempt`), a commit the link stopped waiting for being withdrawn so it never installs later, and the committed bundle is kept apart from the screen for `Screen::restore`. Production selects `SimpleSkin`, whose preparation stages nothing, so its commit only checks the device; selecting a declarative bundle is test-only (`RenderLink::select`).
- The loading presentation (S10-03c, design.md "Rendering and Skins", loading state): the model's loading runtime is portable and drawn by no renderer yet (S10-03c-1, R3); render's `LoadingPresentation` owning it beside the installed skin, its installation before choices are accepted, recovery, memory and typed refusal (S10-03c-2), and the player's loading clock, progress, descriptor, covers and text bridged into `LoadingFrame` (S10-03c-3) are planned. Until then the declarative skin draws nothing of its own while loading (the default `PlaySkin::build_loading`), under the load's lines.

## 7. Compromises and decisions

### What stage 3 ships

| Wish | Ideal | Stage 3 | Later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Other aspects | Reflow per aspect | Anchored regions, background fill, side inset; imports pillarboxed | Per-aspect overrides |
| Player layout | Drag anything | Seven fixed regions, lane width, key-driven adjust mode, numbers in the file; imported skins not adjustable | Mouse drag with `egui`; per-region opacity |
| Judgment-line height | Its own setting | `playfield` y; lift stays a play setting | Revisit if players ask |
| Enlarging | Vector everything | @2x, text at final size | SVG, SDF shapes, MSDF text |
| Shrinking | Perfect | Frame-aware mips, nearest for pixel art | — |
| Resize during play | Re-rasterize | Anchors and fit recomputed; assets refreshed between plays | — |
| Export | Everything | 16:9 1080p `.luaskin`, shared field set, regions as offsets, a report of what was left out | 16:10 and 4:3 bakes, an upstream flag; aspect, mips: never |
| Expressions | General | Typed, range-checked integers, Lua-translatable | Built-ins the conversion report asks for |
| Animation | Curves per key | Four eases per track | Per-key ease and in-out in the authored format, split into keys on export |
| Imports | All existing skins | beatoraja JSON and Lua | LR2 (decision 1) |

### Decisions

1. **LR2 skins.** (a) The LR2 importer right after stage 3. (b) Inside stage 3, also before the authored format. (c) Inside stage 3, after it. **Decision: (a).** beatoraja-family skins are what current players use, and the runtime model is built to grow with its first consumer, so the LR2 importer adds its own semantics (cubic and per-key ease, the LR2 IDs) when it comes.
2. **Where layout adjustments live.** (a) Device scope (follows the monitor and the seat). (b) Player scope. **Decision: (a),** keyed by skin and aspect class; the physical setup is what the adjustments depend on.
3. **Screens narrower than 16:9.** (a) The canvas grows taller. (b) Letterbox. **Decision: (a);** anchors already handle it.
4. **Scratch side.** (a) A player setting that the lanes widget lays out, exported as a one-of option. (b) Not in stage 3. **Decision: (a).**
5. **Name of the format and its file.** "tapestry skin", `skin.toml`.
6. **Fast/slow on the play screen.** (a) Add fast and slow counts to `Tally` in `eval`. (b) Leave them out. **Decision: (a);** it is pure counting in the evaluator.
